=== analysis-1
Find all real numbers $x$ such that $4^x-2^{x+2}+3=0$.
=== analysis-2
Find the smallest positive value of $x$ such that $x^3-9x^2+22x-16=0$.
=== analysis-3
Emma is seated on a train traveling at a speed of $120$ miles per hour. She notices distance markers are placed evenly alongside the track, with a constant distance $x$ between any two consecutive ones, and during a span of $6$ minutes, she sees precisely $11$ markers pass by her. Determine the difference (in miles) between the largest and smallest possible values of $x$.
=== analysis-4
The function $f(x)=x^5-20x^4+ax^3+bx^2+cx+24$ has the interesting property that its roots can be arranged to form an arithmetic sequence. Determine $f(8)$.
=== analysis-5
Determine
$$\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{\sqrt{x+2014}}{\sqrt{x}+\sqrt{x+2014}}.$$
=== analysis-6
Find $f(2)$ given that $f$ is a real-valued function that satisfies the equation
$$4f(x)+\left(\frac{2}{3}\right)(x^2+2)f\left(x-\frac{2}{x}\right)=x^3+1.$$
=== analysis-7
Let $f(x)=x^2+18$ have roots $r_1$ and $r_2$, and let $g(x)=x^2-8x+17$ have roots $r_3$ and $r_4$. If $h(x)=x^4+ax^3+bx^2+cx+d$ has roots $r_1+r_3$, $r_1+r_4$, $r_2+r_3$, and $r_2+r_4$, then find $h(4)$.
=== analysis-8
Suppose an integer-valued function $f$ satisfies
$$\sum_{k=0}^{2n+1}f(k)=\ln|2n+1|-4\ln|2n-1|\quad\text{and}\quad\sum_{k=0}^{2n}f(k)=4e^n-e^{n-1}$$
for all non-negative integers $n$. Determine $\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{f(n)}{2^n}$.
=== analysis-9
Find $\alpha$ such that
$$\lim_{x\to 0^+}x^{\alpha}I(x)=a\quad\text{given}\quad I(x)=\int_0^{\infty}\sqrt{1+t}\cdot e^{-xt}\,dt$$
where $a$ is a nonzero real number.
=== analysis-10
Suppose that $x^3-x+10^{-6}=0$. Suppose that $x_1<x_2<x_3$ are the solutions for $x$. Find the integers $(a,b,c)$ closest to $10^8x_1$, $10^8x_2$, and $10^8x_3$ respectively.
=== geometry-1
Consider a regular hexagon with an incircle. What is the ratio of the area inside the incircle to the area of the hexagon?
=== geometry-2
Regular hexagon $ABCDEF$ has side length $2$ and center $O$. The point $P$ is defined as the intersection of $AC$ and $OB$. Find the area of quadrilateral $OPCD$.
=== geometry-3
Consider an isosceles triangle $ABC$ ($AB=BC$). Let $D$ be on $BC$ such that $AD\perp BC$ and $O$ be a circle with diameter $BC$. Suppose that segment $AD$ intersects circle $O$ at $E$. If $CA=2$ what is $CE$?
=== geometry-4
A cylinder with length $l$ has a radius of $6$ meters, and three spheres with radii $3$, $4$, and $5$ meters are placed inside the cylinder. If the spheres are packed into the cylinder such that $l$ is minimized, determine the length $l$.
=== geometry-5
In a $100$-dimensional hypercube, each edge has length $1$. The box contains $2^{100}+1$ hyperspheres with the same radius $r$. The center of one hypersphere is the center of the hypercube, and it touches all the other spheres. Each of the other hyperspheres is tangent to $100$ faces of the hypercube. Thus, the hyperspheres are tightly packed in the hypercube. Find $r$.
=== geometry-6
Square $ABCD$ has side length $5$ and arc $BD$ with center $A$. $E$ is the midpoint of $AB$ and $CE$ intersects arc $BD$ at $F$. $G$ is placed onto $BC$ such that $FG$ is perpendicular to $BC$. What is the length of $FG$?
=== geometry-7
Consider a parallelogram $ABCD$. $E$ is a point on ray $\overrightarrow{AD}$. $BE$ intersects $AC$ at $F$ and $CD$ at $G$. If $BF=EG$ and $BC=3$, find the length of $AE$.
=== geometry-8
Semicircle $O$ has diameter $AB=12$. Arc $AC=135^\circ$. Let $D$ be the midpoint of arc $AC$. Compute the region bounded by the lines $CD$ and $DB$ and the arc $CB$.
=== geometry-9
Let $ABC$ be a triangle. Construct points $B'$ and $C'$ such that $ACB'$ and $ABC'$ are equilateral triangles that have no overlap with $\triangle ABC$. Let $BB'$ and $CC'$ intersect at $X$. If $AX=3$, $BC=4$, and $CX=5$, find the area of quadrilateral $BCB'C'$.
=== geometry-10
Consider $8$ points that are a knight's move away from the origin (i.e., the eight points $\{(2,1),(2,-1),(1,2),(1,-2),(-1,2),(-1,-2),(-2,1),(-2,-1)\}$). Each point has probability $\frac{1}{2}$ of being visible. What is the expected value of the area of the polygon formed by points that are visible? (If exactly $0$, $1$, $2$ points appear, this area will be zero.)
