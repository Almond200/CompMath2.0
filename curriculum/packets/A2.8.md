# Authoring packet: A2.8, Composite and Inverse Functions

`algebra-2/A2.8` · Year 2 anchor (Lesson 8; not yet reviewed for structure) · Stage S1 (draft for review)

## 1. Narrative arc

The chapter opens with a question: can every rule be undone? Undoing "double, then add 3" means "subtract 3, then halve", so the order reverses.

Composing two simple rules in both orders shows that composition is not commutative and that its domain is a chain of conditions. Trying to undo $x\mapsto x^2$ fails, because two inputs give one output. Restricting the domain repairs it, and the horizontal-line test is uniqueness made visible.

The graph of an inverse is the reflection in $y=x$. The pay-off is a family of problems in which an inverse *value*, or a sum involving $f$ and $f^{-1}$, is found without ever writing the inverse formula. The chapter closes on equations of the form $f(f(x))=x$ and nested radicals, where an increasing function forces $f(x)=x$.

## 2. Learning objectives

1. Compose functions and find the domain of a composite as a chain of conditions.
2. Decide invertibility from injectivity, restricting a domain when needed, and derive an inverse rule.
3. Use the reflection in $y=x$, and the monotonicity of an inverse, to locate values without a formula.
4. Solve equations by recognising a function and its inverse, including $f(f(x))=x$ for increasing $f$.
