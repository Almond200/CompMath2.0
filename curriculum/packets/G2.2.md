# Authoring packet: G2.2, Power of a Point

`geometry-2/G2.2` · Year 2 anchor (Lesson 12) · Stage S4 (manuscript written and every problem verified; awaiting the owner's review in the preview) · Priority pilot (owner, 2026-11-27)

## 1. Narrative arc

Draw any line through a fixed point P and it cuts a circle in two points. The two distances from P change wildly as the line turns, but their product does not. The chapter makes that surprise its question: *why should a product of two lengths be blind to the direction of the line?*

The learner first measures it: a table of chords through one interior point, all with the same product. The inscribed angle theorem they already know then explains it, through a pair of similar triangles. The same pair of triangles works when P is outside the circle, for two secants, and in the limit where a secant becomes a tangent.

Seeing one argument cover three pictures motivates a single number for P: its power, $OP^2 - r^2$, signed so that inside, on and outside are one formula. The chapter ends by using the invariant as an equation-maker. When a figure has two unknown lengths on one line, power gives an equation for each point you choose, and choosing the right point is the skill.

The chapter sets up the rest of the circle sequence. The converse (equal products force a circle) proves concyclicity in G2.3–G2.4. The same similar triangles prove Ptolemy and Stewart in G2.5. The signed power becomes a coordinate expression in G2.6 and the radical axis in G3.3.

## 2. Learning objectives

By the end the learner can:
1. State and prove the intersecting-chords, secant-secant and tangent-secant relations from similar triangles, naming the equal angles and why they are equal.
2. Explain why the three relations are one invariant, and compute the power of a point from the center distance and radius, with its sign.
3. Choose the point whose power to use, and set up one equation per chosen point when lengths are unknown.
4. Recognise a hidden circle-and-line configuration (a chord through a point, a tangent from a point) inside a larger figure.
5. Say when power does **not** decide a length (one equation, two unknowns; points not on one line) and what else is needed.

## 6. Connections to the rest of the circle sequence

- **G2.3 Cyclic Quadrilaterals I.** The "segment products" criterion (G2.3.3) is the converse of G2.2.2 and G2.2.4. G2.2's close poses it as an open question ("if the products are equal, must the four points be concyclic?") and G2.3 answers it. G2.3's Brahmagupta warm-up uses perpendicular chords, which G2.2 does not.
- **G2.4 Cyclic Quadrilaterals II.** G2.4.2 gives the tangent–chord angle its full treatment; G2.2.3 only uses it as a limit. G2.4.4 proves the converse of power with the signed interpretation from G2.2.5.
- **G2.5 Ptolemy and Stewart.** The auxiliary-point proof of Ptolemy reuses G2.2's similar-triangle move; the G2.5 opening should point back to it. Stewart's theorem can also be read as power of the foot of a cevian on a circle through the vertex; this is an optional remark there, not a requirement.
- **G2.6 Lines and Circles.** G2.6.5's $S=(x_0-a)^2+(y_0-b)^2-r^2$ is exactly G2.2.5's signed power. G2.6.8's chord-midpoint locus and G2.6.10's common tangents both reuse G2.2.
- **G3.3 Radical Axes.** Points of equal power with respect to two circles. G2.2.5's sign convention must match G3.3.1's.
- **G3.8 Inversion.** The choice of inversion radius as a power product (G3.8.4).
