# Authoring packet: N2.1, Modular Inverses and the Chinese Remainder Theorem

`number-theory-2/N2.1` · Proposed chapter (no Year 2 source) · Stage S1 (draft for review)

## 1. Narrative arc

A congruence is a whole arithmetic progression. The chapter's question is: *when two progressions with different steps meet, where, and how often?*

It starts with a schedule (two repeating events) and finds the meeting times by listing, which is slow. The listing shows a pattern: once they meet, they meet again every lcm of the steps. Solving "$3x\equiv 2 \pmod 7$" requires dividing by 3, and Bézout supplies the inverse. With inverses, successive substitution builds the meeting point for any number of coprime steps.

Non-coprime steps may never meet, and the compatibility condition says exactly when. The chapter ends with products of consecutive integers modulo a composite number, where the Chinese remainder theorem counts solutions instead of finding them.

## 2. Learning objectives

1. Decide invertibility modulo $m$ from $\gcd(a,m)$ and compute inverses by the Euclidean algorithm.
2. Solve $ax\equiv b\pmod m$ completely, including the no-solution and multiple-solution cases.
3. Construct and verify a CRT solution for coprime moduli by substitution or inverses, and count representatives in an interval.
4. State and use the compatibility condition for non-coprime moduli.
5. Count solutions of polynomial congruences such as $x(x-1)\equiv0$ prime power by prime power.
