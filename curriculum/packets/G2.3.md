# Authoring packet: G2.3, Cyclic Quadrilaterals I

`geometry-2/G2.3` · Year 2 anchor (Lesson 13; not reviewed for structure) · Batch 1 ([plan](../batches/B1-circles.md)) · Stage S4 (written, verified)

## 1. Narrative arc

G2.2 closed with a question: do equal crossing products force a circle? G2.3 answers it, and more generally turns "four points on a circle" from a given into something to prove. Three points always determine a circle (G2.3.1); four points on a circle give equal inscribed angles and supplementary opposite angles (G2.3.2); each property has a converse, and the converses are the tests for a hidden circle, with the crossing-product test answering G2.2 (G2.3.3); proofs combine both directions, working backward from the target (G2.3.4).

## 2. Learning objectives

1. Decide whether points are concyclic, and prove that three non-collinear points determine one circle.
2. Use the opposite-angle and exterior-angle properties, and equal inscribed angles, to move angles and build similar triangles.
3. Prove four points concyclic with the right test: equidistance, a common segment seen at right angles, equal angles, supplementary opposite angles, the exterior angle, crossing products.
4. Find hidden circles in figures with altitudes and right angles, and write short angle-chasing proofs.
