# Authoring packet: A3.3, Advanced Polynomial Structure and Root Problems

`algebra-3/A3.3` · Proposed chapter, Volume III · Stage S1 (draft for review)

## 1. Narrative arc

The roots of most polynomials cannot be written down, yet contest questions about them have exact answers. The chapter's question is: *what can be known about roots that cannot be found?*

It starts with a quantity that looks impossible to compute without the roots, such as $\sum 1/(r+1)$. A substitution turns it into a coefficient of a new polynomial whose roots are the transformed ones. Newton's identities then turn power sums into coefficients.

As Volume III requires, the chapter then removes the cue: the learner must notice that a requested quantity is symmetric, or that a set of outputs is symmetric, before choosing a tool. It closes with the 2026 AIME II #11 refinement (spec refinement 2): proving that a collision among signed sums must occur, then verifying that the case exists.

## 2. Learning objectives

1. Construct the polynomial whose roots are a transformation of given roots (shift, reciprocal, square, sum of pairs) without solving.
2. Use Newton sums and symmetric functions for power sums, with correct initial values.
3. Reconstruct a polynomial from structure: minimal polynomials of radicals, degree arguments, interpolation.
4. Decide when a symmetric expression suffices, and when a collision or existence argument is also needed.
